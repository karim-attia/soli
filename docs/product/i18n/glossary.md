# Soli glossary (all languages)

Created 2026-11-23 (i18n plan `docs/product/i18n/i18n.md`, step 4.1) as a skeleton. Merged 2026-11-23 (steps 5.2 / 5.3): every language's rules, terms, ASO keywords and sources come from the six research notes in `docs/product/i18n/notes/research-r*.md`; the cross-language decisions the orchestrator took afterwards are in "All languages" and are marked "decided 2026-11-23" where they change a researcher's pick.

**This glossary is the INPUT to the translations**, not a description of them: `src/i18n/locales/<locale>.json` and the store text in `store/` conform to it. It is binding for everything a player reads or hears: UI strings, screen-reader labels, share text, store listings, release notes.

- It governs **values, not keys**. i18n keys, code identifiers and file names stay English and technical.
- **Changing a term:** change the row here first (add the date and the reason in the row), then sweep the locale files and store text. A new user-facing concept gets a row here in the same change that introduces it.
- **English is the source of truth** (`src/i18n/locales/en.json`). Every other language is a translated language: nobody types a translation ad hoc, the translator skill does it (`.agents/skills/soli-translation/SKILL.md`). Translators and reviewers do not edit this file; they report, the orchestrator merges.
- **Empty cell** = not decided yet: a translator who needs it stops and reports. **«draft»** after a term = the researcher was unsure. The pick still stands as the decision until a reviewer or Karim changes it; the alternative is in the cell (*alt:*) and in the research note.
- **Where a research note and this glossary differ, the glossary wins.** The notes hold the evidence, the reasoning per term, example phrases and the open doubts; decisions taken after the research are only here.
- In this document «…» around a word is only this document's quoting mark. In sources and keyword tables, [F] / [read] = the page was fetched during the research, [K] / [known] = platform wording from the researcher's knowledge, to be confirmed on a device.
- Invisible direction marks are always written as their JSON escape (`\u200E`, `\u200F`), here and in the locale files, never as the raw character.

## UX-writing principles

### All languages

- **`{{placeholders}}` stay exactly as in English**: same names, same braces, never translated. They may move inside the sentence. Spaces around them follow the target language, except a leading or trailing space of the whole string, which stays.
- **A placeholder stands bare.** `{{card}}` always holds the plain card name (nominative, no article), and nothing is glued to a placeholder: no case ending (ru, pl), no suffix (tr), no particle (ko), no article (fr, it), no prefix (ar, he). Build the sentence around it as a label («Hint: {{card}}, to column {{column}}» in the language's own phrasing). `a11y.rank.*` and `a11y.suit.*` are used only inside `a11y.card`, so they may hold the form the card pattern needs (ru genitive plural suits, tr possessive ranks).
- **Plurals:** a key with `_one` / `_other` in English gets exactly the plural forms of the target language (listed per language below; the jest test is the authority). Every form that can stand for more than one number uses `{{count}}`, never a literal number or a number word. A form that stands for exactly one number in that language (Arabic and Hebrew `one` and `two`) may be written without a digit where that is how the language says it («حركتان», «שני מהלכים»): natural beats literal (decided 2026-11-23).
- **Short words on tight labels, and the length limit wins.** The dock and header buttons, the HUD badges and the caption on the stock have room for about the English length. Keys marked `tight` in `src/i18n/locales/en.context.json` get the short everyday word; the longer explanation belongs in a description line. Where the note gives a number (stock caption ≤ 6 characters, HUD badges ≤ 8, the "{{count}} left" caption ≤ 8 including the number), a term that does not fit loses to the shorter one, also against the researcher's preference (decided 2026-11-23). A tight label without a number (dock and dialog buttons) keeps the researched word and is checked on a small phone.
- **Whole sentences, natural phrasing.** Translate the meaning for a player, not word by word. Word order, punctuation and capitalisation follow the target language, not English (English uses title case on some buttons: "New Game", "Deal Again"; most languages do not). The English " — " between two clauses is never copied: each language's section says what replaces it. The ellipsis is the single character … (U+2026) in every language.
- **Platform words for platform actions.** Undo, Cancel, Share, Settings, Reset, Off: use the word Apple and Google use in that language's system UI. Where the two platforms differ, the language's section picks one word for both. **Undo and Cancel may be the same word** where that is the platform standard (fr «Annuler», it «Annulla», he «ביטול»): the two never share a screen. It is the one accepted exception to "one word per thing" (decided 2026-11-23).
- **No developer jargon.** The player never reads «solver», «catalog», «heuristic», «seed», «algorithm» (see Forbidden words).
- **Names** (decided 2026-11-23):
  - **Soli** is never translated and never transliterated: Latin letters in every script, never declined, no article or prefix glued to it where the language can avoid that.
  - **Klondike:** Latin-script languages keep «Klondike» where English uses it (Turkish: store text only, app sentences say «Solitaire»). Other scripts write it in their own letters: クロンダイク, 클론다이크, كلوندايك, קלונדייק, and 克朗代克 (store description only; in the app Chinese says 纸牌接龙). Russian uses the game's everyday name «Косынка»; «Клондайк» is store text and keywords.
  - **Vegas** (the scoring mode): Latin-script languages keep «Vegas»; other scripts transliterate: ベガス, 维加斯, 베가스, Вегас, فيغاس, וגאס. It reads naturally there and needs no direction marks in Arabic and Hebrew.
  - **Auto Up** is translated descriptively in every language (term tables), never left in English.
  - **A / J / Q / K** on the card faces stay in every language.
- **`$` amounts come from code, in the English position, in every language.** `formatVegasScore` (`src/solitaire/scoring.ts`; the spoken form is in `src/i18n/format.ts`) prints `$` first, no space, and U+2212 for minus: «$52», «−$12», also where local typography would write «52 $». Translators never re-format, re-order or re-type an amount; a literal amount in a sentence («$0») copies that form. The `$` is a literal dollar sign (casino dollars are part of the mode, not a currency setting). Arabic and Hebrew put `\u200E` directly before a `$` amount or its placeholder inside a sentence.
- **Latin digits (0–9) everywhere**, also in Arabic. A number that comes from `{{count}}` is not written as a word (the one exception is under Plurals).
- **Dates, times and number grouping come from code** (`Intl` with the app language and the device region). Never hand-format them in a string.
- **Honest, calm tone.** Soli has no ads, no streaks, no pressure: no exclamation-mark marketing, no «amazing», no urgency. A warning is a quiet notice. Do not make a claim stronger than English does: "No more useful moves" must not become "No moves possible" or "You lost".
- **Never show or assume the player's gender.** No past-tense verb about the player in Russian and Polish («Победа!», «Wygrana!»), verbal nouns on controls in Arabic and Hebrew, no slash forms in any language.
- **One word per thing.** The same term in the app, the screen-reader labels and the store text. Different things never share a word (stock ≠ deck ≠ waste; deal ≠ game where the language can tell them apart). Where the everyday word for the stock and the deck is the same (ru «колода», pl «talia», tr «deste», it «mazzo»), the pile keeps the word and the 52 cards are written as "deck of 52 cards" or "52 cards".
- **"Tip:" is not the Hint word.** The undo tip (`notice.undoTip`) starts with a different word than the Hint button, or with no label at all (de: no label; nl «Tip:» next to «Hint»; fr «Astuce :»; it «Consiglio:»). A translator whose language has one word for both drops the label.
- **Vegas is a scoring mode, not gambling.** No casino, betting or real-money vocabulary in store text in any language (store ratings for simulated gambling, local advertising rules, and it is not what the mode is). Each language lists its words.
- **`about.dedication`:** "my girlfriend" is the language's natural word for a romantic partner, never one that reads as "a female friend". Arabic uses «حبيبتي» (decided 2026-11-23).
- **Store text follows store policy** (see Forbidden words and `store/README.md`), and respects the character limits by rewording, never by abbreviating. Keywords are not a translation: they come from the language's keyword table.

### English (`en`, source)

- American spelling in the app. The en-GB store listing says "Patience" next to "Solitaire"; there is no en-GB app locale.
- Title case on buttons and settings titles as they are today ("New Game", "Deal Again", "Auto Up"); sentence case in descriptions and notices.
- Plural forms: one, other.
### German (`de`): Swiss High German, for everyone

**Rule 1, decided by Karim (2026-11-23) and not open for "correction": German is written in Swiss High German. Always `ss`, never `ß`.** There is ONE German locale (`de`) and ONE German store listing (`de-DE` in both stores); every German-speaking device (de-CH, de-DE, de-AT, …) gets this text. No de-CH / de-DE split.

Why: Soli is made in Switzerland, and Swiss-origin apps ship one German in Swiss spelling:

- Threema: one German with ss everywhere ([threema-android](https://www.github.com/threema-ch/threema-android)).
- Infomaniak kDrive: the in-app German (`values-de`) contains no ß ([android-kDrive](https://github.com/Infomaniak/android-kDrive)).
- SBB, MeteoSwiss, Migros, Coop, Swiss Post, NZZ, 20 Minuten: ss even in the German and Austrian App Store storefronts.
- Swiss firms that think global-first (Proton, Smallpdf, GIANTS) use ß instead. Nobody splits de-CH and de-DE.
- Neither store has a Swiss German listing locale: [App Store localizations](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/app-store-localizations), [Google Play languages](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/table/4419860).

Guards: jest fails on a `ß` in `de.json` (`test/unit/i18n/locales.test.ts`) and in the German store files (`test/unit/store/storeListings.test.ts`).

Decided rules:

- **ss for ß in every word** («Spass», «gross», «schliessen», «heisst», «Strasse»). Not a typo; never change it.
- **Words that every German speaker understands.** The Swiss character shows in the spelling and in "Made in Switzerland", not in dialect or in words Germans and Austrians have to guess. Standard High German vocabulary is fine wherever Swiss usage is the same.
- **Prefer a word without ß/ss contrast where a neutral synonym is just as good**, most of all in store text, so the spelling does not look like a mistake to a German reader before they know the app is Swiss. The list is the table "ss-words to avoid" below. Never bend a sentence out of shape for this: a natural «Spass» beats a stiff workaround.
- **The German store description says "Made in Switzerland"** in a natural closing line, so ss reads as origin.
- **Numbers and dates** come from `Intl` with the device region (de-CH: «1'000», «6. Juli»; de-DE: «1.000»). Never typed into a string.
- **Plural forms:** one, other.
- Nouns are capitalised as German requires; otherwise sentence case («Neues Spiel», not «Neues spiel», and no English title case).

Researched 2026-11-23 (R1), merged with the cross-language decisions the same day. Evidence levels, reasoning per term and open doubts: [research-r1-de-nl.md](notes/research-r1-de-nl.md).

- **Register: du, written lower-case («du», «dein», «dir»).** Never «Sie», never capitalised «Du». Prefer phrasing that needs no address at all (infinitive or noun labels: «Neues Spiel», «Spiel beenden?»); use du where a sentence speaks to the player («Dein laufendes Spiel wird beendet …»). Imperatives in the du form («Zieh den Rückgängig-Knopf zur Seite»). Evidence: the German rules page of FRVR Solitaire is du throughout; the top German solitaire listings mix Sie and du inside one text (Microsoft Solitaire Collection: marketing Sie, subscription text du; «Solitär・»: mostly Sie, some du), which reads as sloppy, so Soli picks one. Swiss utility apps (Twint's store text, SBB, Threema) say Sie, but they are banks and transport, not games; Swisscom's consumer blog is du. A free, personal, one-developer game is du.
- **Name of the game: «Solitär».** That is what Windows called the game in German since Windows 3.0 (de.wikipedia) and what the German store titles say («Solitär・», «Klassisches Solitär», «Solitär - Patience Klondike Karten Spiel»). «Solitaire» (English spelling) is the second most common spelling in German listings and is a store keyword, not an in-app word. «Patience» is the traditional word for the genre («eine Patience legen») and is a keyword too. «Klondike» is the correct variant name; players who use solitaire apps have seen it, everyday players have not: use it only next to «Solitär» («Klondike-Solitär»), never alone as the explanation.
- **Suit names: Herz, Karo, Kreuz, Pik.** Not the Swiss names for French-suited cards (Herz / Ecken / Kreuz / Schaufel). Reasons: (1) glossary rule "words every German speaker understands": «Ecken» and «Schaufel» are unknown in Germany and Austria; (2) Swiss sources themselves give both («Herz, Ecke/Karo, Schaufel/Pik, Kreuz/Treff» in the Jass article; SRF's Jass broadcasts label «Karo (Ecken)»), and the Swiss Jass app SwissJass+ describes its French deck as «Karo, Herz, Pik, Kreuz»; (3) only part of German-speaking Switzerland plays with French cards at all (Bern, Solothurn, Basel, Aargau, Thurgau), the rest uses Rosen / Schilten / Eicheln / Schellen, so the Jass names are not even universal inside Switzerland. The suit names are only spoken by screen readers.
- **Rank names: Ass, Bube, Dame, König.** Not the Jass words («Bauer», «Under», «Ober»). «Ass» has ss in every German spelling: no conflict with rule 1.
- **Card name word order: suit first, no preposition:** «Pik Ass», «Herz Dame», «Karo Sieben» → `{{suit}} {{rank}}`. Never «Ass von Pik».
- **«Farbe» means suit** in German card language. For red/black write «rot» / «schwarz» («abwechselnd rot und schwarz»), never «Farbe wechseln», or the sentence becomes ambiguous.
- **Quotes: Swiss guillemets «…» without inner spaces** (Swiss typography; NZZ, SBB, federal chancellery style). Never „…“ and never "…". Inner quotes ‹…›. Use quotes sparingly; UI labels named in a sentence need none.
- **Apostrophe ’ (U+2019)**, and only where German needs one (almost never: no «Karim’s», no plural apostrophe).
- **Ellipsis: the single character … (U+2026)**, directly after the word («Verlauf wird geladen…»).
- **Dash:** where English has « — » (em dash), German uses the en dash with spaces « – » (U+2013). Applies to the two notices («Keine sinnvollen Züge mehr – mach einen Zug rückgängig oder starte ein neues Spiel.»).
- **No exclamation marks** except a single one in a win message if English has one («Schön gewonnen!»). The not-found screen's "Oops!" is the other exception.
- **Sentence case, German capitalisation.** No English title case («Neues Spiel», «Neu austeilen», «Lösbare Spiele»). Compounds are written as one word («Zugzähler», «Spielverlauf») or with a hyphen when a name is part of it («Vegas-Total», «Klondike-Solitär», «Rückgängig-Knopf»). No "Deppenleerzeichen" («Vegas Total» is wrong).
- **`$` stays in front of the number, no space: «$52», «−$52» as the code formats it.** That is also the Swiss convention (currency sign before the amount); do not move it behind the number as German-German style would.
- **Plural forms: one, other.** Notes for the translator: (1) «Dollar» does not change after a number: `_one` «{{count}} Dollar», `_other` «{{count}} Dollar» (identical on purpose; tell the reviewer). (2) «{{count}} Zug» / «{{count}} Züge», «{{count}} Karte» / «{{count}} Karten», «{{count}} Stunde» / «{{count}} Stunden», «{{count}} Minute» / «{{count}} Minuten», «{{count}} Sekunde» / «{{count}} Sekunden». (3) «{{count}} left» has no noun in English: «Noch {{count}}» in both forms.
- **Words identical to English** that the jest allow-list must accept for `de`: «Vegas», «Standard», «Total», and `{{label}}, {{value}}`-type patterns.
- **Swiss words that are welcome** because every German speaker understands them: «Total» (HUD badge; Germans would write «Gesamt» but read «Total» without effort), «nochmals», «gratis».
- **Swiss words to avoid** (Germans and Austrians stumble): «Ecken», «Schaufel», «Bauer» (for Jack), «innert» (→ «innerhalb»), «allfällig» (→ «möglich», «eventuell»), «Unterbruch» (→ «Unterbrechung», «Pause»), «tönen» (→ «klingen»), «retour» (→ «zurück»), «aufdatieren» (→ «aktualisieren»), «Game» for Spiel (→ «Spiel»), «parkieren», «zügeln».
- **Plain words before patience jargon (2026-11-24, reason: Karim: Blatt/Talon unfamiliar).** Karim speaks German natively, never played solitaire in German, and stumbled over «Blatt» (deal) and «Talon» (stock). Rule: a German speaker who knows no patience vocabulary must understand every in-app word. Changed: deal «Blatt» → **«Spiel»** («Spiel 2345-6789», «Lösbare Spiele», «Dieses Spiel nochmals spielen?»); stock «Talon» → **«Nachziehstapel»**; About screen «Gamification» → «Tricks», reverted the same day (Karim: «Gamification» is fine in German). Kept after the same check: «Abwurfstapel», «Ablagestapel», «Spalte», «Neu austeilen», «Rückgängig», «Tipp», «Automatisch ablegen», «Karten ziehen», «Nicht gelöst», «Streaks» (store text; approved by Karim). Evidence: «Blatt» means the deck in German card language (de.wikipedia Klondike: «Gespielt wird mit einem Blatt»; de.wikipedia Microsoft Solitär: «mit einem Blatt von 52 Karten»), and the same sources call a deal «Partie» or «Kartenverteilung»; cardgames.io's German UI labels a specific deal «Spiel: #234235» / «Nummeriertes Spiel starten»; Solitaire Palast, GIGA and t-online say «Runde». Only FRVR's (translated) page uses «Blatt» for a deal. For the stock: de.wikipedia Microsoft Solitär says «Nachziehstapel», GIGA writes «Talon-Stapel (Nachziehstapel)», FRVR and cardgames.io «Vorratsstapel»; «Talon» is in de.wikipedia Klondike and Solitaire Palast's glossary only. «Ablagestapel» for the foundation is the majority word (de.wikipedia twice, FRVR, GIGA, t-online), but cardgames.io and Solitaire Palast («Ablage») use it for the waste: if it ever confuses, switch to «Zielstapel» (Solitaire Palast). Trade-off of «Spiel»: deal and game merge into one word; that is how players talk, and no string needs the difference.
- **Short labels (tight keys):** «Neues Spiel», «Tipp», «Rückgängig», «Ziehen», «Punkte», «Züge», «Zeit», «Total», «Aus».
- **"Tip:" in the undo tip bubble must not become «Tipp:»**, because «Tipp» is the Hint button (one word per thing). Start the bubble without a label («Zieh den Rückgängig-Knopf zur Seite, um durch dein Spiel zu reisen») or with «Gut zu wissen:».

### French (`fr`)

Status: researched 2026-11-23 (R2), merged with the cross-language decisions the same day. Evidence levels, reasoning per term and open doubts: [research-r2-fr-it.md](notes/research-r2-fr-it.md).

- **2026-11-24 plain-words check** (details: [plainwords-fr-it-nl.md](notes/plainwords-fr-it-nl.md)). No term changed: «donne» (MobilityWare, Brainium; everyday card word from belote and tarot), «pioche», «défausse» are plain French. Wording only: the two notices say «Annulez des coups ou …» (a bare «Annulez» has no object), the hint label is «Indice : piochez.» (was «tirez dans la pioche»), store «séries» → «séries quotidiennes». Open for Karim's eye: «fondation(s)» and «Montée auto» are the two words a non-player may not know (store feature line; alt «Rangement auto»).
- **Register: vous.** Sentences address the player with «vous» (imperative: «Annulez des coups ou commencez une nouvelle partie»). Evidence: the three biggest solitaire apps write their French in «vous» (MobilityWare, Brainium, Microsoft Solitaire Collection), and so do the French system UIs of Apple, Google and Microsoft. Only web portals aimed at a young audience use «tu» (FRVR). «Vous» also reads right for every age in France, Romandie, Belgium and Québec.
- **Buttons and menu entries are infinitives or nouns, never imperatives:** «Annuler», «Partager», «Rejouer», «Jouer», «Tirer», «Noter Soli»; settings titles are nouns («Avertissements», «Compteur de coups»). Alert questions use the infinitive: «Commencer une nouvelle partie ?».
- **Name of the game: «Solitaire».** Every top app in the French stores is titled «Solitaire». «Réussite» (France) and «patience» (Belgium, Switzerland, older usage) are understood by card players but nobody searches an app by them: store keywords only, never in the app. In running text the game is lower-case («une partie de solitaire»); «Soli», «Klondike» and «Vegas» keep their capital.
- **«Klondike» stays «Klondike».** Card-game players know it and the top apps use it in subtitles («Jeu de cartes Klondike»); casual players do not, so store text always puts «solitaire» next to it. Where English says "Klondike" in the app (empty History text, share message), French keeps «Klondike».
- **Neutral French.** No word that is only French, Swiss, Belgian or Canadian. Digits are never written as words (so no «septante» / «soixante-dix» question). Numbers, dates and times come from `Intl`.
- **Spaces before punctuation:** a no-break space (U+00A0) before `:` `?` `!` `;` and inside guillemets (`«` NBSP … NBSP `»`). Never a normal space (it wraps the mark onto the next line) and not the narrow U+202F (font coverage is not guaranteed; Apple's, Google's and Microsoft's French UIs use U+00A0). No space before `,` `.` `…`. This also applies before a colon that follows a placeholder or a word in a label («Indice : …», «Astuce : …»).
- **Quotes:** « » (with NBSP inside). **Apostrophe:** typographic ’ (U+2019) everywhere («l’historique», «aujourd’hui», «d’annulation»). **Ellipsis:** the single character … (U+2026), no space before it. **Ligature:** «cœur» with œ, never «coeur».
- **Dash:** English joins two clauses with " — ". French does not copy it: write two sentences or use a colon («Aucun coup utile restant. Annulez ou commencez une nouvelle partie.»).
- **Capitalisation:** sentence case everywhere («Nouvelle partie», not «Nouvelle Partie»). Capitals keep their accent («À», «É»: «Égalité», «À propos»).
- **`$` in Vegas:** stays where English has it (`$0`, `$52`), because the HUD formats the amount in code that way. Not «52 $». Spoken form (screen reader): «{{count}} dollar» / «{{count}} dollars», negative «moins {{count}} dollars».
- **Plural forms (CLDR, checked by jest):** one, many, other. **`_one` covers 0 and 1** («0 coup», «1 coup», «2 coups»), so `_one` always shows `{{count}}` and its noun is singular. `_many` is required and equals `_other`. Agreement to watch: «{{count}} restant» (one) / «{{count}} restants» (many, other), which refers to «passage» (masculine).
- **Gender of the board words** (for agreement in screen-reader labels): la carte, la pioche, la défausse, la fondation, la colonne, la donne, la partie (feminine); le coup, le passage, le tableau, le score, le total (masculine). «vide» is the same for both.
- **Card names:** `{{rank}} de {{suit}}`, suit in the singular: «As de pique», «Dame de cœur», «Huit de carreau». The four suit names start with a consonant, so «de» never elides. Rank names are written with a capital in their own strings (they start the label); suit names are lower-case.
- **No article in front of `{{card}}`.** A card name comes without article, so do not build «déplacez {{card}}» (it would read «déplacez Roi de pique»). Write the hint as a label: «Indice : {{card}} vers la colonne {{column}}.»
- **«plus» is ambiguous in writing** («plus de coups» = no more / more moves). Never start the notice with «Plus de …»: use «Aucun … restant» or «Il ne reste aucun …».

### Spanish (`es`)

Status: researched 2026-11-23 (R3), merged with the cross-language decisions the same day. Evidence levels, reasoning per term and open doubts: [research-r3-es-ptBR.md](notes/research-r3-es-ptBR.md).

- **Register: tú.** Every top solitaire listing read addresses the player with tú («Roba 1 o roba 3 cartas», «Toca o arrastra cartas», «Pon orden en las cartas»). Tips and notices use the tú imperative («Arrastra…», «Deshaz…»); buttons and menu entries use the infinitive («Deshacer», «Compartir», «Jugar»). Never usted, never vosotros or ustedes (address one player), never voseo.
- **Name of the game: «Solitario».** It is the everyday name in Spain and Latin America and what the stores use. «Klondike» is known to enthusiasts and appears in listings as «solitario Klondike»; use it in store text and where English names the variant, always next to «solitario» on first mention. «Paciencia» exists (Wikipedia: «solitario de la paciencia») but nobody searches for it. **«Carta Blanca» is NOT this game**: it is the Spanish name of FreeCell.
- **Neutral vocabulary.** Pick the word both sides of the Atlantic use: «dispositivo» or «teléfono» (not «móvil» / «celular»), «tomar» or «robar» (never «coger», vulgar in much of Latin America), no «ordenador» / «computadora», no «vale», no regional slang. Where Spain and Latin America differ and no neutral word exists, the term table decides.
- **French-suited cards, not the Spanish deck.** Suits are «corazones», «diamantes», «tréboles», «picas»; face cards «jota», «reina», «rey». Never «oros / copas / espadas / bastos» or «sota / caballo»: those belong to the baraja española.
- **Orthography and punctuation.** Opening «¿» and «¡» always («¿Empezar una partida nueva?»). Soli's calm tone means almost no exclamation marks at all. No space before `? ! : ;`. Sentence case: only the first word and proper names are capitalised («Nueva partida», «Cartas a robar», not «Nueva Partida»). Accents on capitals («ÚLTIMA»): the HUD shows labels in uppercase. English " — " inside a notice becomes a colon or a full stop, not a dash («No quedan jugadas útiles: deshaz o empieza otra partida.»).
- **Quotes, apostrophes, ellipsis:** curly double quotes “…” (what Apple's and Google's Spanish UI use; «…» is correct in print but looks bookish in an app). Ellipsis is the single character `…`, attached to the word («Cargando historial…»). No apostrophes in Spanish.
- **`$` and numbers:** the `$` stays in front as in English (`$52`, `−$17`): that is the Latin American convention and the mode's casino look; Spain would write «52 $», accepted loss. Spoken form: «{{count}} dólar / dólares», negative «menos {{count}} dólares». Grouping and decimals come from `Intl`.
- **Plural forms (CLDR, checked by jest):** one, many, other. `_one` is exactly 1; 0 takes `_other` («0 jugadas»); `_many` is required and equals `_other`.
- **Gender agreement:** «partida» is feminine, so its states are «Resuelta», «Incompleta». Stat tiles count games: «Resueltas», «Incompletas».
- **Short labels:** «Nueva partida» (New Game), «Pista» (Hint), «Deshacer» (Undo), «Robar» (Draw), «Puntos» (Score), «Jugadas» (Moves), «Tiempo» (Time), «Total», «No» (Off), «Quedan {{count}}» ({{count}} left).
- **Also decided:** «Hola» (drawer entry "Hello"), «Hecho por» ("Made by"), `_one` of "{{count}} left" «Queda {{count}}»; `about.dedication`: "my girlfriend" = «mi novia».
- **Plain words (2026-11-24 plain-words check).** Deal «reparto» → **«partida»** («Partida 2345-6789», «Partidas con solución», «¿Reintentar esta partida?»). In everyday Spanish «reparto» is a delivery, a film's cast or the act of dealing, not one specific game; cardgames.io's Spanish UI labels a deal «Juego: #234235», Brainium says «Partidas ganadoras»; only MobilityWare's listing has «Repartos que se pueden ganar». Bonus: the History row «Partida … · Resuelta» now agrees in gender. Trade-off: deal and game are one word (as German «Spiel»); no string needs the difference. The button stays «Repartir». Win message «¡Buena victoria!» → «¡Bien jugado!» (literal → idiomatic). Kept after the same check: «robar», «mazo», «descarte», «base», «pasada», «jugada», «pista», «Jugabilidad», «gamificación», «Subida automática», «rachas» (store). Notes: [plainwords-es-ptBR.md](notes/plainwords-es-ptBR.md).

Status: researched 2026-11-23 (R3), merged with the cross-language decisions the same day. Evidence levels, reasoning per term and open doubts: [research-r3-es-ptBR.md](notes/research-r3-es-ptBR.md).

- **Register: você.** All Brazilian listings read address the player with você («Você pode treinar seu cérebro», «Você vai precisar comprar cartas do monte»). Tips and notices use the você imperative («Arraste…», «Desfaça…»); buttons and menu entries use the infinitive («Desfazer», «Compartilhar», «Jogar»). Never tu, never «o senhor / a senhora», no plural address. Possessive «seu / sua».
- **Name of the game: «Paciência».** It is the Brazilian name since Windows («Paciência»), Microsoft's Brazilian listing says «jogos clássicos de Paciência», and it is the main term on Portuguese Wikipedia («Paciência, também conhecido como Solitário»). «Solitário» is the name in Portugal and is understood in Brazil; «Solitaire» is typed a lot in the Brazilian stores. So: «Paciência» in the app and as the store name's game word, «Solitário» and «Solitaire» in the store subtitle / keywords so pt-PT players and searchers find it. «Klondike» is known to enthusiasts; store text says «Paciência Klondike».
- **Brazilian spelling and vocabulary** (2009 orthographic agreement as used in Brazil): «tela», «compartilhar», «configurações», «anúncios», «cronômetro», gerund («carregando»). pt-PT readers understand all of it; do not mix in European forms («ecrã», «partilhar», «definições», «anular», «a carregar»).
- **French-suited cards:** suits «copas», «ouros», «paus», «espadas»; face cards «valete», «dama», «rei»; «ás» with accent.
- **False friend with Spanish:** Portuguese «naipe» is the SUIT (Spanish «naipe» is a card); Portuguese «espadas» is ♠ (Spanish says «picas»). Never copy between the two languages.
- **Orthography and punctuation.** No inverted marks. No space before `? ! : ;`. Sentence case: only the first word and proper names capitalised («Novo jogo», «Dar as cartas»). Accents on capitals («PONTUAÇÃO»). English " — " may stay as a spaced travessão « — » or, better in a short notice, become a colon or full stop.
- **Quotes, apostrophes, ellipsis:** curly double quotes “…” (Brazilian standard). Ellipsis is the single character `…`, attached to the word («Carregando histórico…»).
- **`$` and numbers:** `$` in front as in English (`$52`, `−$17`); not «R$», not «US$». Spoken form: «{{count}} dólar / dólares», negative «menos {{count}} dólares». Grouping and decimals come from `Intl`.

### Russian (`ru`)

Status: researched 2026-11-23 (R4), merged with the cross-language decisions the same day. Evidence levels, reasoning per term and open doubts: [research-r4-ru-pl-tr.md](notes/research-r4-ru-pl-tr.md).

### Arabic (`ar`)

Status: researched 2026-11-23 (R6), merged with the cross-language decisions the same day. Evidence levels, reasoning per term and open doubts: [research-r6-ar-he.md](notes/research-r6-ar-he.md).

- **Variety:** Modern Standard Arabic, understood from Morocco to the Gulf. No dialect words in the app (no «كوتشينة», «شدّة», «بلوت», «شايب», «بنت»); regional words are store keywords only.
- **Register and gender:**
  - **Labels, buttons, menu entries, settings titles, hints: the verbal noun (masdar)**, which has no gender: «تراجع», «مشاركة», «إلغاء», «سحب», «إعادة المحاولة», «تلميح: نقل {{card}} إلى العمود {{column}}».   - **Sentences that address the player:** first choice is a form that is written the same for a man and a woman: «يمكنك …», «لعبتك», «رصيدك» (the suffix ـك has no written gender without vowel marks), or an impersonal sentence. Where only an imperative reads naturally (store description, a tip), use the **masculine singular imperative** («اسحب», «جرّب», «العب»): it is the convention in every Arabic listing and app read. Never the feminine, never slash forms («اسحب/ي»), never the plural, never «قم بـ…» + masdar (Microsoft guide: use the plain form).
  - No vowel marks (tashkeel), with two exceptions: the tanween on an accusative that would otherwise be misread («مجانًا», «دولارًا») and a shadda where a word is ambiguous without it. Never add a vowel mark to ـك.
- **Name of the game:** «سوليتير». The variant is «سوليتير كلوندايك» (spelling «كلوندايك», not «كلونديك»); players know the word only next to «سوليتير», so the app says «سوليتير» alone wherever English allows and «كلوندايك» only where English says Klondike. «لعبة الصبر» (Patience) exists in descriptions but nobody uses it as the name. «الشدّة» / «الكوتشينة» / «الورق» are regional words for playing cards, not for this game.
- **Orthography:** correct hamza (أ إ آ ؤ ئ), ة not ه at the end of feminine nouns, ي not ى for final yā («في», «التي»), no kashida (ـ) for stretching, no Persian or Urdu letters.
- **Punctuation:** Arabic comma «،», semicolon «؛», question mark «؟». Period, colon, exclamation mark and parentheses as in English. Space after punctuation, none before. The English em dash in notices ("No more useful moves — undo or…") becomes a period and a second sentence, or « – » (en dash with spaces); not an em dash.
- **Quotes, apostrophes, ellipsis:** avoid quotes; if one is needed, straight `"…"` (Microsoft Arabic guide). Ellipsis is the single character `…` at the end of the string, as in English.
- **Digits:** Western digits 0–9 in every string, in the store text too (rule "All languages"; Microsoft Arabic guide: Western digits are the standard; the Arabic store listings read use them). Dates, times and grouped numbers come from code with `-u-nu-latn`: without it `ar-SA` and `ar-EG` print «١٬٢٣٤» while `ar` and `ar-AE` print «1,234».
- **Direction marks (bidi).** The text is right-to-left; "Soli", digits and `$` amounts are left-to-right islands. Rules:
  1. **"Soli" is the only Latin word** in Arabic text. Vegas and Klondike are written in Arabic letters («فيغاس», «كلوندايك») so that they need no marks. *(Decided 2026-11-23 for every non-Latin script, see "All languages".)*
  2. **Arabic prefixes never attach to "Soli"** and it never takes «الـ»: write «في Soli», «على Soli», «من Soli»; never «بـSoli», «لـSoli», «وSoli».
  3. **Never start a string, a line of store text or a paragraph with "Soli"** or another Latin token: apps that pick the direction from the first strong character (share targets, the stores, some text views) would lay the whole line out left-to-right. Reword so an Arabic word comes first; if that is impossible, start the string with `\u200F`. **Exception (decided 2026-11-24): the store name (`name.txt` / `title.txt`) is «Soli: …», brand first and without any mark**, like the other 13 listings and like most Latin-brand apps in the Arabic and Hebrew storefronts («Snake.io: لعبة الثعبان», «Spider solitaire - العاب ورق»); the line reads correctly in both a right-to-left and a first-strong layout.
  4. **A dollar amount inside a sentence is preceded by `\u200E`**: `"فيغاس \u200E{{value}}"`, `"من \u200E$0"`. Without it Arabic shows «52$» (the `$` jumps to the other side after an Arabic letter) and a negative amount shows its minus on the wrong side. Strings that consist of the amount alone (HUD badge) need nothing.
  5. **Put the Arabic word before a bare number** where the order is free: «بقي {{count}}», not «{{count}} بقي».
  6. No other direction characters, ever (no U+202A–U+202E, no isolates typed by hand). Parentheses and the period at the end mirror by themselves.

### Hebrew (`he`)

Status: researched 2026-11-23 (R6), merged with the cross-language decisions the same day. Evidence levels, reasoning per term and open doubts: [research-r6-ar-he.md](notes/research-r6-ar-he.md).

- **Register and gender: gender-neutral, no slash forms.** Hebrew verbs and adjectives show the reader's gender, so:
  - **Buttons, labels, menu entries, settings titles, hints: the verbal noun (שם פעולה)**: «ביטול», «שיתוף», «איפוס», «משיכה», «רמז: העברת {{card}} לטור {{column}}». Microsoft's Hebrew guide prescribes exactly this for UI elements («שמירה», not «שמור» / «לשמור»); Google's Hebrew UI avoids the imperative for the same reason.
  - **Sentences:** «אפשר ל…» / «ניתן ל…» + infinitive («אפשר לבטל מהלך או להתחיל משחק חדש»), or words that are written the same for both genders: «שלך», «לך», «ברצונך», past tense («ניצחת»). A question to the player may use the plural («מוכנים לסיבוב נוסף?»).
  - Last resort where nothing neutral reads well: second person **plural** («גררו», «שחקו»), which Microsoft's guide uses for instructions. Never the masculine singular imperative («בטל», «משוך», «לחץ»), never slash forms («לחץ/י»).
  - Adjectives that describe a game agree with «משחק» (masculine): «נפתר», «פעיל».
- **Name of the game:** «סוליטר» (Hebrew Wikipedia's article name; «סוליטייר» is a common second spelling, keyword only). «קלונדייק» is known as the Windows game (Hebrew Wikipedia says so) and is used where English says Klondike. «פסיאנס» is the old name (Patience), keyword only.
- **Orthography:** no niqqud; full spelling (כתיב מלא) as the Academy prescribes: «הכול», «ניקוד», «שנייה». Hebrew prefixes (ב, ל, מ, ה, ו, ש, כ) before a Latin word or a digit take a hyphen: «ב-Soli», «ל-3». Plain hyphen-minus, no maqaf (־).
- **Punctuation:** as in English (`, . : ? !`). The English em dash in notices becomes a period and a second sentence, or « – » (en dash with spaces).
- **Quotes, apostrophes, ellipsis:** avoid quotes; if one is needed, straight `"…"`. Abbreviations use the Hebrew marks gershayim `״` (U+05F4) and geresh `׳` (U+05F3), never an ASCII quote («סה״כ»). Ellipsis is the single character `…`.
- **Digits:** 0–9 (Hebrew never uses anything else). `Intl` prints «23 בנוב׳ 2026, 14:05».
- **Direction marks (bidi).** Same model as Arabic, but simpler because Hebrew does not move the `$`:
  1. **"Soli" is the only Latin word.** Vegas and Klondike are written in Hebrew letters («וגאס», «קלונדייק»). *(Decided 2026-11-23 for every non-Latin script, see "All languages".)*
  2. **Never start a string, a store-text line or a paragraph with "Soli"**; reword, or start with `\u200F` if there is no other way. **Exception (decided 2026-11-24): the store name (`name.txt` / `title.txt`) is «Soli: …», brand first and without any mark**, like the other 13 listings and like most Latin-brand apps in the Arabic and Hebrew storefronts («Snake.io: لعبة الثعبان», «Spider solitaire - العاب ورق»); the line reads correctly in both a right-to-left and a first-strong layout.
  3. **A dollar amount that can be negative is preceded by `\u200E` inside a sentence**: `"וגאס \u200E{{value}}"`. A positive `$52` or `$0` renders correctly without it; add the mark anyway so both languages follow one rule.
  4. Put the Hebrew word before a bare number where the order is free («נותרו {{count}}»).
  5. No other direction characters.
- **Plural forms (CLDR, checked by jest):** one, two, other. (`many` was removed from Hebrew in CLDR 42. A phone with older system data may still answer `many` for 20, 30, …; i18next then falls back to `_other`, so no `_many` key is written.)
